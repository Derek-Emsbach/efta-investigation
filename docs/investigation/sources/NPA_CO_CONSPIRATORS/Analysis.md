# NPA Co-Conspirators: Sarah Kellen, Nadia Marcinkova, Adriana Ross

## Deep-Read Analysis — March 2026

---

## 1. Overview

On September 24, 2007, the United States Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Florida signed a Non-Prosecution Agreement (NPA) with Jeffrey Epstein. Buried in paragraph 5 of that agreement was a provision that would define the trajectory of the entire case:

> "The United States also agrees that it will not institute any criminal charges against any potential co-conspirators of Epstein, **including but not limited to Sarah Kellen, Adriana Ross, Lesley Groff, or Nadia Marcinkova**."

Four women — named explicitly in a federal agreement — received blanket immunity from prosecution for their roles in a sex trafficking operation involving 40+ identified minor victims. This analysis focuses on three of the four: Sarah Kellen, Nadia Marcinkova, and Adriana Ross. (Lesley Groff has been analyzed separately in `docs/investigation/sources/LESLEY_GROFF/Analysis.md`.)

All three were named in a sealed 53-page federal indictment drafted by May 2007. All three were identified as co-conspirators in an 82-page prosecution memorandum. None was ever charged with a federal crime.

## 2. The NPA Immunity Provision

### Source Documents
- **EFTA01186070** (pp. 14–17): Jane Doe 1 & 2 CVRA Motion for Summary Judgment — contains exact NPA language
- **EFTA01186086** (¶40): Final NPA text naming all four co-conspirators

### Key Facts

The immunity provision evolved during September 2007 negotiations:

**Defense proposal (Sept 16, 2007):** "Epstein's fulfilling the terms and conditions of the Agreement also precludes the initiation of any and all criminal charges which might otherwise in the future be brought against Sarah Kellen, Adriana Ross, Lesley Groff, and Nadia Marcinkova **or any employee of N.E.S.** for any criminal charge that arises out of the ongoing federal investigation."

**Final NPA text (Sept 24, 2007):** "The United States also agrees that it will not institute any criminal charges against **any potential co-conspirators** of Epstein, including but not limited to Sarah Kellen, Adrian Ross, Lesley Groff, or Nadia Marcinkova."

The final version was actually broader than the defense proposal: it covered "any potential co-conspirators," not just the four named women. The phrase "including but not limited to" made the four names illustrative, not exhaustive.

The NPA was kept secret from all victims. Defense counsel Jay Lefkowitz explicitly requested: "Please do whatever you can to keep this from becoming public." The agreement contained a confidentiality provision preventing disclosure. Victims were sent letters saying the case was "under investigation" — nine months after the deal was signed.

## 6. The Sealed 53-Page Indictment

By May 2007, AUSA Marie Villafaña had drafted both an 82-page prosecution memorandum and a 53-page sealed indictment. The indictment named 19 Jane Does and included Kellen as a defendant with specific overt acts.

On September 19, 2007, five days before the NPA was signed, Villafaña emailed defense counsel: "I have had an 82-page pros memo and 53-page indictment sitting on the shelf since May to engage in these negotiations. There has to be an ending date."

The existence of a 53-page indictment — fully drafted, ready for grand jury presentation — demonstrates that the government had assembled sufficient evidence for prosecution. The NPA did not reflect a weak case. It reflected a political decision to abandon a strong one.

In June 2008, Villafaña was still preparing to present the indictment to a grand jury ("Operation Leap Year"), including New York victims and new evidence about the Brunel/MC2 modeling pipeline. Grand jury subpoenas were issued. But Epstein's state guilty plea on June 30, 2008, activated the NPA and terminated the federal case.

## 11. Open Questions

1. **What was in the 53-page sealed indictment?** Only fragments have been disclosed. The full indictment — naming specific defendants, overt acts, and victim counts — would establish exactly what crimes were immunized.

2. **Did Kellen, Marcinkova, or Ross provide substantive cooperation in exchange for immunity?** Ross gave a proffer. The NPA does not require cooperation from any co-conspirator. Were they debriefed? Did they provide information about others in the network?

3. **What happened to the physical evidence Ross destroyed?** The FBI chart says she removed items from both Palm Beach and the Virgin Islands. What items? Were any recovered? Did the destruction materially impair the investigation?

4. **Why was Marcinkova given immunity rather than treated as a victim?** If purchased from her family at age 14 and brought to the US by Epstein, she was a trafficking victim. The NPA treats her as a co-conspirator deserving immunity — not as a victim deserving protection.

5. **What is Marcinkova's current status?** Reported missing since early January 2024. No confirmed public sightings. Given her unique position as both victim and immunized co-conspirator, her disappearance warrants investigation.

6. **Why did the NPA use "including but not limited to"?** This language extends immunity beyond the four named women to any "potential co-conspirator." How many additional people were effectively immunized by this provision?

7. **What was discussed on AUSA Villafaña's personal email?** On September 17, 2007, she asked defense counsel to send documents to her "home e-mail address." The CVRA filing notes the government produced no emails from prosecutors' personal accounts in discovery.

8. **What did Ross know about Prince Andrew?** Her invocation of the Fifth Amendment when questioned about Andrew suggests knowledge that, if disclosed, would be self-incriminating — even with NPA immunity in place.

## 13. Analytical Framework: The Architecture of Disposability

The NPA co-conspirator provision reveals a specific prosecutorial logic: these four women were treated as extensions of Epstein rather than as independent actors with independent criminal liability.

**The hierarchy of accountability:**
- Epstein: pleaded guilty to state charges, served 13 months
- Maxwell: convicted in 2021, sentenced to 20 years (14 years after the NPA)
- Kellen, Marcinkova, Ross, Groff: blanket federal immunity, never charged
- 40+ identified victims: not consulted, not informed, labeled "prostitutes" in state charges

**Why these four and not Maxwell?** Maxwell was not named in the NPA — a notable omission. The four named women were Epstein's employees and direct subordinates. Maxwell was his social equal and co-architect. The NPA immunized the operational staff while leaving the strategic partner exposed — not out of principled distinction, but because Maxwell's defense team was not at the table.

**The victim-perpetrator paradox (Marcinkova):** Marcinkova was simultaneously a trafficking victim (purchased at ~14, brought to the US, used as a "sex slave") and a perpetrator (participated in the abuse of other minors). The NPA resolved this paradox by granting immunity — treating her as a co-conspirator who might be charged, rather than as a victim who should be protected. This framing served Epstein's interests: if Marcinkova was a co-conspirator, she could be silenced by immunity rather than empowered by victim status.

**The evidence destruction problem (Ross):** Ross's admission that she destroyed evidence from both the Palm Beach and Virgin Islands properties during the investigation should have been a standalone federal charge (obstruction of justice, 18 U.S.C. § 1519). Instead, the NPA immunized this conduct. A woman who admitted destroying evidence in a child sex trafficking investigation received the same immunity as the women who scheduled the abuse.

**The structural function of immunity:** The NPA's co-conspirator provision did not merely prevent prosecution — it prevented testimony. By immunizing co-conspirators, the government eliminated any leverage to compel their cooperation against Epstein or others in the network. Kellen, who managed scheduling for 10+ victims, could never be compelled to testify about who else received appointments. Ross, who destroyed evidence, could never be compelled to describe what was destroyed. The immunity was not a concession to the defense — it was a lock on the vault.

The four women named in the NPA were not the architects of the trafficking operation. They were its workforce. The NPA immunized the workforce to protect the architecture.
