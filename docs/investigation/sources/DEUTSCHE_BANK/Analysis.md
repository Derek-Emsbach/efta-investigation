## 1. Overview

Deutsche Bank maintained a banking relationship with Jeffrey Epstein and related entities from August 2013 to December 2018. During this period, the bank opened 76 accounts for Epstein-controlled entities, processed millions of dollars in suspicious transactions — including payments to named co-conspirators, "ostensible foreign models," and victim settlement attorneys — and failed to implement or communicate its own compliance conditions.

On July 6, 2020, the New York State Department of Financial Services imposed a **$150 million penalty** on Deutsche Bank in the first enforcement action by any regulator against a financial institution for dealings with Epstein.

The primary document — EFTA01681865 — is Deutsche Bank's own 52-page forensic accounting presentation to SDNY prosecutors, delivered September 12, 2019, two months after Epstein's arrest. It contains 19 exhibits (A through S) mapping the complete financial infrastructure: every account, every shell company, every payment to co-conspirators, models, high-profile individuals, and settlement attorneys.

## 2. How Deutsche Bank Onboarded a Convicted Sex Offender

### The Recruitment (Spring 2013)

A Deutsche Bank relationship manager — identified in the NYDFS consent order as "RELATIONSHIP MANAGER-1" and in internal documents as **Paul Morris** — had previously worked at JP Morgan on the Epstein account team. After joining Deutsche Bank in November 2012, Morris proposed Epstein as a client, projecting "$100-300 [million] overtime... w/ revenue of $2-4 million annually."

Morris suggested all accounts be opened under "entities" affiliated with Epstein, "not personal accounts" — a structure that would obscure the beneficial owner behind shell company names.

### The Approval Email (May 5, 2013)

EXECUTIVE-1 (the co-head of Wealth Management Americas) sent an email stating he had "spoke with" the Head of AML Compliance and the General Counsel (who chaired the Americas Reputational Risk Committee). "Neither suggest [the Epstein relationship] requires rep risk and we can move ahead so long as nothing further is identified through KYC and AML client adoptions."

The NYDFS found:
- No record of this conversation exists beyond the email itself
- The ARRC never formally met to review the onboarding
- An AML compliance officer approved the relationship based solely on this secondhand email

### The Onboarding Memo

The memo prepared for EXECUTIVE-1 stated plainly:
- "Epstein was charged with soliciting an underage prostitution in 2007"
- "He served 13 months out of his 18 month sentence"
- "He was accused of paying young woman for massages in his Florida home"
- He was involved in 17 out-of-court civil settlements

**The bank onboarded him anyway. The first accounts opened August 19, 2013.**

## 9. The ARRC Review: Compliance Theater

### January 2015 Escalation

AML Officer Dmitri Saks (EFTA01418996) conducted Enhanced Due Diligence on Southern Financial LLC. His findings:
- Epstein is sole UBO, registered sex offender
- "By 2011, 40 underage girls had come forward with testimony of Epstein sexually assaulting them"
- "Epstein managed to settle at least 17 lawsuits out of court"

**Saks' recommendation:** "I approve this account with the condition to mark the relationship as High Risk and the recommendation to be monitored for suspicious activity. However, the material negative information... may pose reputational risk to DB. Therefore, I recommend escalating this case to the Americas Reputational Risk Committee for review and decision."

### The Home Visit (January 22, 2015)

EXECUTIVE-1 and Paul Morris visited Epstein at his New York home. EXECUTIVE-1 "asked Mr. Epstein about the veracity of the recent allegations and appeared to be satisfied by Mr. Epstein's response."

**No contemporaneous records of this meeting exist.** The bank took a convicted sex offender's word for it.

### ARRC Meeting (January 30, 2015)

- Committee met to discuss the relationship
- **No minutes were taken** (violating Deutsche Bank's own policy)
- A member emailed EXECUTIVE-1 that the committee was "comfortable with things continuing" and noted "a number of sizable deals recently"

### The Three Conditions (Never Communicated)

Head of Compliance Elizabeth J. Ford emailed three conditions:
1. Transactions must not be "unusual and/or suspicious activity or are in a size that is unusually significant or novel in structure"
2. New accounts could open only if already approved by Wealth Management
3. The business must "monitor for any further developments" in reputational risk

**Critical failures:**
- Conditions were communicated to senior personnel up to the CEO of Americas
- They were **never communicated to the Epstein relationship team**
- AML OFFICER-2 misinterpreted "unusual" to mean "unusual compared to prior Epstein activity"
- A monitoring team member responded to an alert about payments to a Russian model: **"Once this type of activity is normal for this client it is not deemed suspicious"**

## 14. The NYDFS Consent Order: Key Findings

The $150 million penalty (EFTA00151495) found:

1. **Failure to tailor monitoring to known risks**: "Although the Bank properly classified Mr. Epstein as high-risk, the Bank failed to scrutinize the activity in the accounts for the kinds of activity that were obviously implicated by Mr. Epstein's past."

2. **Compliance conditions never transmitted**: "These mandatory conditions were communicated to several senior Bank personnel, up to and including the Bank's CEO of the Americas. Inexplicably, however, they were apparently never communicated to all members of the Epstein relationship team."

3. **Misinterpretation codified ignorance**: The compliance officer "interpreted the clause 'transactions [with] unusual and/or suspicious activity' to mean transactions that were unusual, suspicious, or novel as compared to the prior history of transactions related to the Epstein relationship."

4. **Monitoring reduced to age checks**: "AML OFFICER-2 only instructed the relevant transaction monitoring team to verify, using internet searches, that any woman involved with transactions related to the Epstein relationship was at least 18 years old."

5. **Reference letters to other banks**: "Despite the Bank's decision to offboard all Epstein accounts due to reputational risks, RELATIONSHIP MANAGER-2 drafted reference letters to two other financial institutions, on Deutsche Bank letterhead, indicating in one such letter that he was 'unaware of any problems relating to the operation or use of [the] accounts.'"

## 18. Analytical Framework: The Architecture of Willful Blindness

The Deutsche Bank-Epstein relationship demonstrates a five-layer compliance failure:

**Layer 1 — Revenue Over Risk:** A relationship manager recruited a convicted sex offender by projecting $2-4M annual revenue. The onboarding memo acknowledged the criminal history. The bank proceeded.

**Layer 2 — Approval by Hearsay:** The entire relationship was approved based on a single email claiming a casual conversation occurred. No formal committee review. No minutes. No due diligence report.

**Layer 3 — Conditions Without Communication:** When compliance finally imposed conditions, they were never transmitted to the people who needed to implement them. The relationship team continued operating as before.

**Layer 4 — Redefining "Suspicious":** A compliance officer reinterpreted "unusual activity" to mean "unusual compared to what Epstein normally does." Payments to Russian models became normal. Payments to co-conspirators became normal. $200,000/year in cash became normal.

**Layer 5 — Clean Exit Letters:** Even after terminating the relationship for reputational risk, the bank wrote reference letters to Epstein's next banks stating it was "unaware of any problems." The willful blindness was exported.

This was not a failure of detection. Deutsche Bank detected everything — the sex offender status, the co-conspirator payments, the model payments, the cash structuring. The failure was the systematic decision, at every level, to continue anyway.

The $150 million fine represents 0.3% of Deutsche Bank's 2019 revenue. The Epstein relationship generated an estimated $8-20M+ in revenue over five years. The fine was a cost of doing business.
