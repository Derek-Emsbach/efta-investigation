## 2. Trust Structure

**Type**: Revocable inter vivos (living) trust. Epstein retained full control during his lifetime:
- All income paid to him (Section 1.1)
- Principal distributed at his written direction (Section 1.2)
- Right to amend in whole or in part (Section 1.3A)
- Right to remove any trustee unilaterally (Section 1.3B)
- Right to revoke the entire trust (Section 1.3C)
- Right to withdraw any principal (Section 1.3D)
- Right to add property (Section 1.3E)

**Governing Law**: Territory of the United States Virgin Islands (Section 8.3). Courts of Delaware AND USVI have "primary supervision." Trustees have discretion to transfer situs to any other country or jurisdiction after Epstein's death without court approval.

**Amendment History**: This is an "Amendment and Restatement" — it replaces a prior version. Corpus search confirms:
- **EFTA01266380**: Original "Jeffrey E. Epstein 2014 Trust" dated November 18, 2014
- **EFTA01266403**: This document — amendment dated May 1, 2015
- **EFTA01266427**: "First Amendment to the Amendment and Restatement" (post-May 2015)

The sequential Bates numbers (01266380 → 01266403 → 01266427) suggest these were produced together from the same file.

**Trust Duration**: Subject to USVI Rule Against Perpetuities (Section 5.9). Trusts continue until "the day preceding the expiration of the maximum period permitted."

### Trustee Powers

Extraordinarily broad. 18 enumerated investment powers (Section 6.3A) and 12 enumerated administrative powers (Section 6.4), including:
- Take possession of all trust assets and collect income (6.3A.1)
- Invest without any statutory investment law limitations (6.3A.1)
- Buy/sell options, warrants, puts, calls (6.3A.4)
- Create or invest in entities (6.3A.10)
- Employ agents and delegate authority (6.4A)
- Delegate check-signing, securities transfer, safe deposit box access (6.4B)
- Transfer trust situs to any other jurisdiction without court approval (8.3)

Self-dealing restrictions (6.3B-D): No trustee may buy from/sell to themselves, sell trust property to themselves as trustee of another trust, or lend trust funds to themselves. However, Section 6.3A.12 permits "good faith transactions" with trustees individually or their entities.

Majority rule: "If more than two Trustees are empowered to participate in the decision... a majority of such Trustees shall be empowered to make such decision" (Section 6.1). With 3 trustees, any 2 can act.

### Exculpation (Section 7.6)

> "No one serving as the Trustee of any trust under this Agreement shall be liable for any loss or depreciation in value occasioned by reason of any negligence, error or mistake of judgment... unless such Trustee has acted in bad faith."

### Resignation (Section 7.3)

> "Any Trustee may resign at any time, without the approval of any court, by an instrument in writing filed with the trust records."

## 4. Trust Property (Schedule A)

**SCHEDULE A IS NOT INCLUDED IN THE DOCUMENT.**

The preamble states: "the property described in Schedule A annexed hereto and made a part hereof." However, the 24-page document contains no Schedule A. This is significant because:

1. The trust property — the assets actually placed into the trust — is unknown from this document alone
2. The bequests in Article II distribute specific properties (NYC townhouse, Little St. James, Zorro Ranch, Paris apartment, Palm Beach house), suggesting these were trust assets
3. The missing schedule means we cannot determine the full scope of assets under trustee control
4. Schedule A may have been deliberately excluded from the production, or it may be a separate document not yet identified in the corpus

## 6. Key Provisions

### Spendthrift Clause (Section 5.5)
Trust assets cannot be pledged, assigned, or seized by creditors of beneficiaries. This includes claims for alimony or support obligations. Does NOT apply to Epstein himself during his lifetime.

### No-Contest Clause (Section 8.5)
Any beneficiary who contests the trust loses their bequest — distributions made "as if such Beneficiary and his issue in any degree had all failed to survive me."

### Situs Transfer Power (Section 8.3)
After Epstein's death, trustees can transfer the trust to ANY other country or jurisdiction without court approval. This is an offshore escape hatch.

### Foreign Court Provision (Section 8.3)
"If both a United States court and a foreign court are able to exercise primary supervision over the administration of the trust, the trust meets the court test." Clearly anticipates dual-jurisdiction scenarios.
