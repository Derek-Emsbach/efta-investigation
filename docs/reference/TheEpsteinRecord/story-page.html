Key Finding

The DANY prosecution file contains documentary evidence of $158 million in payments from Black to Epstein, routed through multiple entities, alongside communications suggesting Black had awareness of Epstein's conduct — yet prosecutors declined to bring charges. The file itself constitutes the strongest evidence of prosecutorial failure in Dataset 12.

The Wire Transfers

Federal banking records obtained by DANY investigators document a systematic pattern of wire transfers that accelerated in 2014 and 2015. The transfers show a clear evolution from relatively modest advisory fees — payments of $2–5 million that might plausibly constitute compensation for financial planning — to transfers in the tens of millions that no reasonable observer could attribute to tax advice.

The redactions in the financial summary are themselves revealing. Note that the total confirmed amount — $158 million — is visible, but additional payments through an unidentified channel have been redacted. Under our classification framework, this represents a Category D redaction: the obscured information appears to protect the scope of the financial relationship rather than any legitimate privacy interest.

Redaction Analysis
The financial summary document (EFTA02731623) contains two targeted redactions within the payment section. The first obscures an additional dollar amount; the second obscures the channel through which those payments were made. Both redactions appear on the same page where the confirmed $158 million total is left visible — suggesting the redacted amounts and channel are more sensitive than the already-staggering confirmed total.
